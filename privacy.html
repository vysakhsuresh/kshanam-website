Privacy policy

Written to describe what this website and this studio actually do, in plain language. If something here turns out to be wrong, that is a bug and we would like to know.

Last updated 3 October 2026

The short version

This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and has no advertising. It does not track you between pages or between visits. Two third parties see a record of your visit because of how the page is built: our host, and Google, who serve the fonts. Details below.

Cookies and local storage

This site sets none. There is nothing to consent to, which is why you have not been shown a banner. If that changes — if we add analytics or a booking system that needs them — this page will be updated before the change goes live, and a notice will appear.

Hosting and server logs

The site is served as static files by GitHub Pages. Like any web server, it records requests: your IP address, the time, the page requested and your browser’s user agent string. We do not have access to an analytics dashboard built from these logs and we do not use them to build any profile of you. They exist for security and abuse prevention on the host’s side, under GitHub’s own privacy terms.

Fonts

The typefaces on this site are served by Google Fonts from fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com. Loading them means your browser makes a request to Google, and Google therefore receives your IP address and user agent. Google states that it does not use Google Fonts requests to build advertising profiles, but the request does leave your browser and we would rather say so than let you find out from a network tab.

The enquiry form

The form on the contact page does not currently post anything to a server. When you submit it, the details are assembled into an email in your own mail application, and nothing reaches us until you press send yourself. We never see an abandoned enquiry.

When you do send it, we hold what you wrote — your name, your email address, the date and location, and anything you told us about the day. We keep enquiries for two years so that we can recognise you if you come back, and we delete them after that. We do not add enquiry addresses to a mailing list, because we do not have one.

If you book us

A booking means we hold rather more: contact details for you and sometimes for family members or other suppliers, the schedule for the day, and the invoicing records we are required to keep.

Photographs are the significant one. We keep the files from a wedding for five years, on encrypted drives and in encrypted cloud backup, so that you can come back to us for a reprint long after you have lost your own copy. Your private gallery link is unlisted and not indexed by search engines, and you can ask us to take it offline at any time.

We only publish photographs — here, or anywhere else — where the contract says we may. If you tell us no, nothing goes online, and that answer can be changed later in either direction. If something of yours is already published and you want it removed, write to us and it comes down; we do not treat a signed release as a reason to argue.

Who else sees your data

No advertiser, no data broker, nobody who pays us for it. The only third parties involved are the ones needed to do the work: our email provider, our cloud backup provider, the gallery platform that hosts your private gallery, our accountant, and — where we have agreed it with you in advance — an associate photographer or film unit working on your wedding.

What you can ask us to do

Write to hello@kshanam.in and you can ask us to tell you everything we hold about you, correct it, send you a copy, take a published photograph down, or delete your records entirely. We will do it within thirty days and we will not ask you why.

The one limit: where we are legally required to keep an invoicing record, that record stays even after the rest is deleted.

Children

Children appear in wedding photographs constantly. We treat images of identifiable children as needing a parent’s agreement before publication, separately from the couple’s own release, and we will take any such photograph down on a parent’s request without discussion.

Changes

If we start collecting something new, this page is updated in the same change that introduces it, not afterwards. The date at the top tells you when it last moved.
